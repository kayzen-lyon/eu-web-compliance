# AI literacy (Article 4)

An organisational duty of the website operator. It produces no code; the agent mentions it once in the report so the owner can act on it.

## How to use this file

1. It applies when the site uses at least one AI system and the operator is not a private individual acting outside any professional activity (profile: any `ai.*` answer `true`, `operator.activity_kind` not `personal`).
2. Put the short text of section 2 in the report, under the AI domain, as an action for the owner. Do not present it as something the code has fixed.
3. Date of application: [../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) section 2.

## 1. The rule

- [ ] Providers and deployers take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and of other persons operating or using AI systems on their behalf [AI Act art. 4]
- [ ] As amended, the duty does not require guaranteeing a specific level of AI literacy for any individual: it is a duty to take measures, proportionate to the people concerned and the systems used [AI Act art. 4]
- [ ] Contractors and agencies that run the AI feature for the operator (for example the agency that configures the chatbot or the freelancer who writes AI-assisted articles) are persons operating it on the operator's behalf [AI Act art. 4]
- [ ] Where the operator publishes AI-generated content under Article 50(4), the Code of Practice asks signatories to make the people who label content aware of when labelling is required, how to apply it, and how to correct a missing label [AI CoP section 2, measure 2.2]

## 2. What to tell the owner

Illustration (adapt to the features found; do not copy as is):

> Your site uses [the support chatbot / AI-written articles / AI product photos]. Under Article 4 of the AI Act, your organisation must take measures so that the people who configure, supervise or use these tools on your behalf understand them well enough: what the tool can and cannot do, when its output must be checked or labelled, and how visitors' data flows through it. A short internal guide and a briefing for the people concerned is a reasonable start; keep a dated record of what you did. This is your organisation's responsibility; the code of the site cannot fulfil it.

Useful content for that guide, all `[best practice]` unless stated:

- [ ] Which AI tools the organisation uses on the site, who may change their configuration, and who answers visitors' questions about them [best practice]
- [ ] That outputs can be wrong or invented, and which ones a person checks before publication (for public-interest text, the fact-check that the editorial exception requires: [transparency.md](transparency.md) section B4) [AI Act Guidelines point 134]
- [ ] When content must carry the AI label and how to apply it ([transparency.md](transparency.md) section B) [AI CoP section 2, measure 2.2]
- [ ] Which data may not be pasted into a third-party AI tool (personal data of customers, confidential information), with a pointer to the privacy policy [best practice]
