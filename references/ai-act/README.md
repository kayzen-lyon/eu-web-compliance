# AI Act for websites

What Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act) asks of a website that uses AI, and what this skill does about it. Written for an agent that builds or changes a site; no legal background is assumed.

## How to use this file

1. Read the summary below, then open only the file that matches your task (table further down). Each file starts with its own "how to use".
2. Dates of application, transition periods and penalty amounts are not repeated anywhere in this folder. They live in [../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) section 2. Read them there when you need them, and quote them from there.
3. Tags in square brackets: `AI Act art. N` is the Regulation itself (binding). `AI Act Guidelines point N` is the Commission Guidelines on Article 50, C(2026) 5054 final: **non-binding**, they say how the Commission reads the text. `AI CoP` is the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content: **voluntary**; adhering to it is not conclusive evidence of compliance ([../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) section 2.2), and its measures are never obligations. `[best practice]` has no official source and is never an obligation.
4. When the answer depends on a qualification you cannot make from the code (provider or deployer, deep fake or not, high-risk or not, prohibited or not), say so, record it as "to confirm", and tell the user to ask a professional. Do not guess in either direction: telling an owner that an obligation applies when it does not costs them money; telling them nothing applies when it does exposes them to fines and misleads their visitors.
5. Work like a careful colleague, not a gate: when the user is about to do something these files warn against, say so plainly, explain the rule in one or two sentences, propose the compliant alternative, and carry on with what can safely be done. Only the stop cases (prohibited practice, high-risk area) halt work on the feature concerned.

## What applies to an ordinary website, in one paragraph

Most websites that "use AI" are **deployers**: they embed a chatbot, call a model through an API, or publish text and images made with an AI tool, and someone else built the system. A deployer's own transparency duties under Article 50 are narrow: tell people when they are exposed to emotion recognition or biometric categorisation (50(3)), and label **deep fakes** and **AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest** (50(4)), unless the text went through real human editorial review. The duty to tell visitors they are talking to an AI (50(1)) and the duty to mark generated content in a machine-readable way (50(2)) belong to the **provider**; a site becomes a provider when it puts a system into service under its own name, for example a chatbot it built itself on a model API, or a model it fine-tuned and released. Whatever the role, this skill always shows a clear "you are chatting with an AI" notice and a visible label on published AI content that falls under 50(4), because they cost little, protect visitors, and keep the site safe if its role was misjudged. Article 5 prohibitions and Annex III high-risk areas are stop cases: the skill does not build those features and refers the owner to a lawyer. AI literacy (Article 4) is an organisational duty of the operator, not code. Article 50 is already applicable; see [../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) section 2 for the exact dates, including the single transition period and the dates of the newest prohibitions.

Source code written with an AI tool is not content that Article 50(2) asks to mark (Guidelines point 68): do not add "generated by AI" comments, headers or labels to code, markup or configuration because of the AI Act.

## What this folder does not cover

- The obligations of providers of general-purpose AI models (Chapter V), of high-risk systems (Chapter III) and of importers and distributors. If the project is one of those, this skill is the wrong tool: say so.
- Sector rules (finance, health, media law, elections) and national law beyond what [../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) states.
- Copyright on training data or generated works.

Everything produced with these files is produced with the help of an AI. It is neither an audit nor legal advice.
