## 1. Accessibility

Scope of the rubric: Transposition of the European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882), market surveillance authority, penalties, national technical standard if any, content of the accessibility information or statement.

Partly verified.

- Transposition law: Gesetz zur Umsetzung der Richtlinie (EU) 2019/882 über die Barrierefreiheitsanforderungen für Produkte und Dienstleistungen (Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz, BFSG). Read: § 1 (purpose and scope; paragraph 2 lists products placed on the market after 28 June 2025) and § 37 (administrative offences). Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bfsg/__1.html and https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bfsg/__37.html
- Not verified: the list of services in scope (rest of § 1), the market surveillance authority, the amounts of fines, the content of the information duty for services, any national technical standard.

## 2. Cookies and other trackers

Scope of the rubric: Law transposing Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC, competent authority, national guidelines on consent banners and audience measurement.

Partly verified.

- Law: Telekommunikation-Digitale-Dienste-Datenschutz-Gesetz (TDDDG), § 25. Storing information in, or accessing information stored in, the end user's terminal equipment is only allowed with consent given on the basis of clear and comprehensive information; information and consent follow the GDPR (§ 25(1)). No consent is needed when the sole purpose is carrying out the transmission of a message over a public telecommunications network, or when the storage or access is strictly necessary for the provider of a digital service to provide a service explicitly requested by the user (§ 25(2)). Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html
- Not verified: the competent authority, national guidelines on consent banners and audience measurement.

## 4. Mandatory identification of the website

Scope of the rubric: Information a website must publish about its operator (imprint, legal notice), beyond Article 5 of Directive 2000/31/EC.

Partly verified.

- Law: Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz (DDG), § 5 "Allgemeine Informationspflichten" (the page is commonly called "Impressum"). Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html
- Who: providers of digital services offered on a business basis, as a rule for remuneration. The information must be easily recognisable, directly accessible and permanently available (§ 5(1)).
- What (paraphrase of § 5(1), points 1 to 8; the German text prevails):
  1. name and address where the provider is established; for legal persons also the legal form, the person authorised to represent it and, if information on the capital is given, the share capital and any unpaid contributions;
  2. details allowing rapid electronic contact and direct communication, including the email address;
  3. the competent supervisory authority, where the activity needs an official authorisation;
  4. the commercial register or similar register and the registration number;
  5. for regulated professions: the professional chamber, the statutory professional title and the State where it was awarded, the professional rules and how to access them;
  6. the VAT identification number or the business identification number (Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer), where the provider has one;
  7. for an AG, KGaA or GmbH in winding-up or liquidation, a statement to that effect;
  8. for audiovisual media service providers, the Member State of establishment and the competent regulatory and supervisory authorities.
- § 5(2): further information duties under other laws remain. Use [../../templates/consumer/legal-notice-eu.md](../../templates/consumer/legal-notice-eu.md), titled in German, and add points 1, 6, 7 and 8 where they apply.
- Not verified: the further duties of other laws that § 5(2) preserves.
