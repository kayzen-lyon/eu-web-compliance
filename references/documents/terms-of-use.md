# Terms of use: drafting

The terms of use (conditions générales d'utilisation) govern the use of the service itself: access, account, conduct, content, moderation, suspension, liability. Sales are governed by the terms of sale ([terms-of-sale.md](terms-of-sale.md)). Template: [terms-of-use-fr.md](../../templates/documents/terms-of-use-fr.md).

## How to use this file

1. Check whether terms of use are needed (section 1) and whether the service hosts user content: then the Digital Services Act fixes part of their content ([../consumer/dsa.md](../consumer/dsa.md)).
2. Collect the answers of section 2 from the owner; the agent writes the structure and leaves the choices to the owner.
3. Apply sections 3 and 4 to every clause. Clauses that limit the user's rights are the ones that most often end up void or abusive.

## 1. When they are needed

- [ ] Outside the cases below, no rule read by this skill requires terms of use; recommend them when the site has accounts or an online service, as the contract that sets the rules of use, and say it is a recommendation [best practice]
- [ ] A service that stores content supplied by its users states in its terms any restriction on that content, including the moderation policies, procedures, measures and tools (automated and human) and the internal complaint rules, in clear and plain language, publicly available and machine-readable [DSA art. 14]
- [ ] Users are informed of any significant change to those terms [DSA art. 14(2)]
- [ ] A service mainly used by or aimed at minors explains its conditions and restrictions in a way minors understand [DSA art. 14(3)]
- [ ] A service in scope of the European Accessibility Act gives, in its general terms or an equivalent document, the information on how it meets the accessibility requirements ([accessibility-information-eaa.md](../../templates/accessibility-information-eaa.md)) [EAA]

## 2. What to collect

- Description of the service, as the owner describes it.
- Access conditions: who may use it, minimum age if any, account creation, verification steps.
- Conduct and content rules: what is forbidden; how reports are handled; what moderation exists (only what really exists).
- User content: who owns it, which rights the operator needs to host and show it, for how long.
- Suspension and closure: by the operator (grounds, notice, statement of reasons) and by the user (how, what happens to data).
- Liability: the limits the owner wants, to be checked by a lawyer.
- Acceptance: the mechanism that really exists (checkbox at sign-up, link next to a button), confirmed by the owner.
- Governing law and disputes; how changes are notified.

## 3. Clauses the agent never writes as such

- [ ] An acceptance mechanism is described only as confirmed by the owner; otherwise the clause carries `[À CONFIRMER : mécanisme d'acceptation]`, and a separate unchecked checkbox is offered as a recommendation in the answer [best practice]
- [ ] Accepting the terms by a checkbox does not make every clause valid: a clause that creates a significant imbalance to the detriment of a consumer is abusive [C. conso L. 212-1]
- [ ] No clause excludes all liability of the operator; a limitation is written "dans les limites autorisées par la loi" and its scope is left to the owner's lawyer [best practice]
- [ ] No clause lets the operator change the terms "at any time without notice" for a service used by consumers; the change procedure distinguishes the kind of change (law, service, price, substantial change) [best practice]
- [ ] No clause gives exclusive jurisdiction to a court of the operator's choice against consumers, and no governing-law clause removes the protection of the law of the consumer's residence; for an operator established outside France or targeting several countries, these clauses are left to a professional [best practice]
- [ ] Account deletion offered because an app store requires it is described as a feature of the service, not as a legal obligation; the person's rights over their data are in the privacy policy [GDPR art. 17(1)]
- [ ] A rule of an app store or a sign-in provider is never written as a legal duty of the operator or the user [best practice]
- [ ] Moderation measures are described only as they exist; the agent does not invent automated filters, response times or appeal steps [DSA art. 14]

## 4. Consistency

- [ ] The operator's identity and contact are those of the legal notice [best practice]
- [ ] Personal data: one sentence and a link to the privacy policy; the terms do not restate or contradict it [best practice]
- [ ] Where a paid offer exists, the sale clauses (price, withdrawal, termination) are in the terms of sale or a separated part, not scattered in the terms of use [best practice]
- [ ] A free service with paid features planned: the terms of sale are drafted when the paid offer exists; the answer says "no sale identified so far" [best practice]

## 5. Review checklist

- [ ] The moderation section exists when user content is hosted [DSA art. 14]
- [ ] No acceptance mechanism, moderation step or feature is described without confirmation [best practice]
- [ ] No exclusion of all liability, no change without notice, no exclusive court against consumers [best practice]
- [ ] Each remaining placeholder is listed in the answer [best practice]
