# Data processing agreement: drafting

The contract the GDPR requires between a controller and a processor (data processing agreement, DPA, accord de sous-traitance). This file covers the case where the operator of the site is the processor for its business clients (a software service that stores its clients' contacts, employees or customers). The opposite case, the operator using its own processors (hosting, email delivery), is in [../privacy/third-parties-and-transfers.md](../privacy/third-parties-and-transfers.md) section 2. ## How to use this file

1. Check that the case applies (section 1), processing by processing. Most operators are controllers for some processing and processors for other processing.
2. Collect the facts of section 2; the mandatory clauses are in section 3 (facts in [../regulatory-status.md](../regulatory-status.md) section 3.1).
3. A DPA is a contract between businesses, not a page for the public. Ask whether it is a model to be accepted by every client, or an annex to one client's existing contract.

## 1. Does the case apply

- [ ] A DPA is needed where the operator processes personal data on behalf of a controller; it is not needed for processing the operator carries out for its own purposes (its own customers, its own newsletter) [GDPR art. 28(3)]
- [ ] The qualification is made processing by processing: the same software service is usually controller for its clients' accounts, billing, security and its own statistics, and processor for the data its clients put into the tool; never "all client data = processing on behalf" in one block [best practice]
- [ ] Data the operator also uses for its own purposes (product improvement, statistics, even aggregated) are a separate processing where the operator decides purposes; this is flagged to the owner's adviser, not hidden in the DPA [best practice]
- [ ] A vendor's role is never decided from its brand: one provider can be a processor for one product (cloud hosting) and act for its own purposes in another (an analytics or advertising product); qualify each service used [best practice]
- [ ] Special categories (health, sick leave records, for example in a human resources tool) or children's data in the client's data are a stop-and-refer case ([../scoping/out-of-scope.md](../scoping/out-of-scope.md)) [GDPR art. 9(1)]

## 2. What to collect

- Who is controller and who is processor, for which processing.
- Subject matter and duration; nature and purpose of the processing; types of personal data; categories of data subjects.
- Sub-processors actually used (hosting, email delivery, support tools, payroll or payment providers), each with its location; never an example list.
- Transfers outside the EU in the chain, and the safeguard of each.
- Technical and organisational measures actually in place (for the annex).
- The breach notification channel and any contractual time the parties agree.
- What happens to the data at the end: deletion or return, and how.
- Whether an audit procedure already exists.

## 3. Mandatory content

- [ ] The contract sets out the subject matter and duration, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data, the categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the controller [GDPR art. 28(3)]
- [ ] The processor processes the data only on documented instructions, including for transfers outside the EU, unless required by law, in which case it informs the controller first unless that law forbids it [GDPR art. 28(3)(a)]
- [ ] Persons authorised to process the data are bound by confidentiality [GDPR art. 28(3)(b)]
- [ ] The processor takes the security measures of Article 32 [GDPR art. 28(3)(c)]
- [ ] The processor engages another processor only with the controller's prior written authorisation, specific or general; under a general authorisation it informs the controller of changes, who may object; it imposes the same obligations on the sub-processor and remains fully liable for it [GDPR art. 28(2); GDPR art. 28(4)]
- [ ] The processor helps the controller answer requests from data subjects exercising their rights [GDPR art. 28(3)(e)]
- [ ] The processor helps the controller with security, breach notification, impact assessment and prior consultation (Articles 32 to 36) [GDPR art. 28(3)(f)]
- [ ] At the end of the services, the processor deletes or returns all the data, at the controller's choice, and deletes existing copies unless the law requires storage [GDPR art. 28(3)(g)]
- [ ] The processor makes available the information needed to demonstrate compliance and allows and contributes to audits, including inspections, by the controller or an auditor it mandates [GDPR art. 28(3)(h)]
- [ ] The processor immediately informs the controller if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes data protection law [GDPR art. 28(3)]
- [ ] The processor notifies the controller of a personal data breach without undue delay after becoming aware of it [GDPR art. 33(2)]
- [ ] The contract is in writing, which includes electronic form [GDPR art. 28(9)]

## 4. What the agent never fills

- [ ] The list of sub-processors, their entities and locations: placeholders until the owner gives the real list [best practice]
- [ ] The security measures annex: placeholders until the owner describes the measures in place; never a list of plausible measures presented as real [best practice]
- [ ] A contractual breach notification time ("within 48 hours"): only the time the parties choose; otherwise the clause repeats the legal standard "dans les meilleurs délais" and leaves the time as a placeholder [GDPR art. 33(2)]
- [ ] The transfer mechanism of each transfer: as confirmed by the owner [GDPR art. 44]
- [ ] A statement that the processor or its sub-processors are "GDPR compliant" or "certified" [best practice]

## 5. Review checklist

- [ ] Each point (a) to (h) of Article 28(3) has a clause [GDPR art. 28(3)]
- [ ] The description of the processing (subject, duration, nature, purpose, data, persons) is specific to the service, not generic [GDPR art. 28(3)]
- [ ] Sub-processors and transfers are the real ones, or visible placeholders [best practice]
- [ ] The DPA is consistent with the privacy policy: what the operator does as controller is in the policy, what it does as processor is in the DPA [best practice]
